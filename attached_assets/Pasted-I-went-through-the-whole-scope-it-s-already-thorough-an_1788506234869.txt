I went through the whole scope — it's already thorough and well-structured. Here's where I'd push further to make it feel genuinely different from the average mining/trading site, building on what's already planned rather than repeating it:

Trust that goes further than a badge

A responsible-sourcing hub specific to your minerals. Tantalite, Coltan and Gold sit squarely in the "3TG" conflict-minerals category (tin, tantalum, tungsten, gold) that OECD- and Dodd-Frank-style due-diligence frameworks track, and Cobalt sourcing gets similar scrutiny. Most mining sites have a vague "sustainability" page — turn the RESPONSIBILITY section into a real due-diligence walkthrough (chain-of-custody approach, KYC/AML posture, alignment with recognized frameworks), using only what the company can actually verify. That's a far sharper signal to smelters, refiners and OEM buyers who have to justify their supply chain internally.
Verifiable, not just displayed, credentials. Take the Trust/Verification Area a step further: each real license or certificate gets a QR code or direct link to the issuing body's registry, so a buyer's compliance team can check it themselves instead of just trusting a badge image.

Make enquiries feel like trading, not lead-gen

An RFQ wizard instead of a plain form. A short guided flow — commodity → grade/spec → quantity → Incoterm → destination port → documentation needed → contact — produces a structured, sales-ready enquiry instead of a vague message, and reads like a real trading desk rather than a brochure site.
A per-commodity spec sheet, auto-generated. Sits right next to your Commodity Showcase cards: buyer picks Copper Cathode, clicks "Download Spec Sheet," gets an instantly generated branded one-pager built from the same verified data already on the page — no extra content to maintain.
An Incoterms / logistics explainer. A short interactive breakdown of FOB / CIF / CFR / EXW as it applies to your actual delivery process. It runs naturally alongside the origin-to-destination journey animation but answers "who's responsible for what, and when" rather than just showing the process — something very few competitor sites bother explaining, even though it's central to every real deal.

Signals of an active market player, not a static brochure

A live reference price strip. A small, clearly-labeled ticker with public reference prices (LME copper/cobalt, spot gold) — not your quoted prices, just market context. Pulled from a public metals API, so it doesn't touch the "no unverified claims" rule since it's public data, clearly marked as indicative.
"Where to meet us" — a trade events list. Commodities trading runs heavily on in-person relationships (Mining Indaba, LME Week, regional conferences). A simple, occasionally-updated list of upcoming events the company will attend bridges the site to real deal-making — a detail almost no competitor thinks to include.
Local time + response expectation on the contact widget. A small touch next to the WhatsApp CTA — HQ local time, "usually responds within X hours." Reduces the hesitation an international buyer feels reaching out across time zones.

One bigger swing, worth flagging even though it's past Release 1

An AI-assisted commodity concierge. A small chat widget trained on your catalogue and FAQs that can answer "do you supply X grade of copper cathode" or "which ports do you ship from" instantly, then hand qualified conversations to WhatsApp. It's the one idea here that isn't purely static — it needs a lightweight function calling an LLM — so it fits better as a fast-follow once the core site is live rather than Release 1.
